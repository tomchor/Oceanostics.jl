Kinetic energy equation

The KineticEnergyEquation module provides diagnostics for every term in the resolved kinetic energy (KE) budget. The kinetic energy per unit mass is defined as

\[e_k = \tfrac{1}{2} u_i u_i\]

and its prognostic equation is obtained by contracting the momentum equation with the velocity:

\[\partial_t e_k = \underbrace{-u_i \partial_j(u_i u_j)}_{\text{advection}} - \underbrace{u_i \partial_j \tau_{ij}}_{\text{stress}} - \underbrace{u_i \partial_i p}_{\text{pressure}} + \underbrace{u_i b_i}_{\text{buoyancy}} + \underbrace{u_i F_{u_i}}_{\text{forcing}}\]

where $\tau_{ij}$ is the viscous/subgrid momentum flux (negative of the stress tensor), $p$ is pressure, $b_i$ is the buoyancy acceleration component in the $i$-th direction, and $F_{u_i}$ is the forcing on the $i$-th momentum equation. As throughout Oceanostics, the lower-case $e_k$ is the pointwise energy density and the upper-case $E_k = \int e_k \, \mathrm{d}V$ its volume integral; the potential energy page states the convention in full.

This decomposition is essential for understanding how kinetic energy is generated (e.g. by buoyancy production or forcing), redistributed (by advection or pressure work), and removed (by viscous dissipation). The module also provides two formulations of the dissipation rate: a general one based on the full momentum flux ($\varepsilon_k = -\partial_j u_i \, \tau_{ij}$), and an isotropic version ($\varepsilon_k = 2\nu S_{ij} S_{ij}$) valid when the turbulence closure uses a single scalar viscosity. On a NonhydrostaticModel the two agree for a constant viscosity, and both are non-negative for a down-gradient closure; on a hydrostatic model only the general one is a term of the budget, as discussed below. As in the momentum equation, $\tau_{ij}$ is the momentum flux Oceananigans' kernels carry (i.e. , the negative of the stress tensor; $-2\nu S_{ij}$ for a constant viscosity).

All diagnostics are computed at (Center, Center, Center).

Hydrostatic models

A HydrostaticFreeSurfaceModel steps $u$ and $v$ and diagnoses $w$ from continuity, so on such a model the kinetic energy is $e_k = \tfrac{1}{2}(u^2 + v^2)$, and KineticEnergy, KineticEnergyTendency, KineticEnergyAdvection, KineticEnergyStress, KineticEnergyForcing, KineticEnergyPressureRedistribution and KineticEnergyDissipationRate all sum over $u$ and $v$ only. The momentum fluxes inside the stress term and the dissipation rate are still computed with the diagnosed $w$, as in the model's own tendencies. Buoyancy does not appear in the hydrostatic momentum equations. It enters through the hydrostatic pressure anomaly $p'$, with $\partial_z p' = b$, so the budget reads

\[\partial_t e_k = -u_i (\boldsymbol{u} \cdot \nabla) u_i - u_i \partial_j \tau_{ij} - u_i \partial_i p' + u_i F_{u_i} , \qquad i = 1, 2 ,\]

cell by cell, apart from the work of the barotropic pressure gradient, which an ImplicitFreeSurface or a SplitExplicitFreeSurface treats implicitly and KineticEnergyTendency therefore leaves out. The conversion $wb$ sits inside the pressure work,

\[-u_i \partial_i p' = -\partial_j (u_j p') + w b , \qquad i = 1, 2 ,\]

which is why PotentialToKineticEnergyConversion uses the diagnosed $w$ on a hydrostatic model. A NonhydrostaticModel that carries a hydrostatic pressure anomaly (Oceananigans' default when there is a buoyancy model and the vertical direction is bounded) is arranged the same way: its $w$ equation has no buoyancy term, and $wb$ reaches the budget through $w \partial_z p'$ instead.

The dissipation rate of this budget is the viscous work above less a transport divergence,

\[-u_i \partial_j \tau_{ij} = -\partial_j (u_i \tau_{ij}) - \varepsilon_k , \qquad \varepsilon_k = -(\tau_{1j} \partial_j u + \tau_{2j} \partial_j v) ,\]

and KineticEnergyDissipationRate computes exactly that, the first two rows of the general form, so $\int \varepsilon_k \, \mathrm{d}V$ is the viscous sink of $\tfrac{1}{2}(u^2 + v^2)$; for a constant isotropic viscosity it equals $\nu \int (|\nabla u|^2 + |\nabla v|^2) \, \mathrm{d}V$. Pointwise, its sign depends on the closure's formulation. A horizontal or vertical formulation (HorizontalScalarDiffusivity, VerticalScalarDiffusivity, CATKE) builds $\tau$ from $u$ and $v$ alone, and $\varepsilon_k$ is then non-negative cell by cell: $\nu_h (\delta^2 + \zeta^2)$ from the horizontal divergence and vorticity, plus $\nu_z [(\partial_z u)^2 + (\partial_z v)^2]$. A three-dimensional ScalarDiffusivity puts $\partial_x w$ into $\tau_{13}$. In the full budget the cross term $\nu \, \partial_z u \, \partial_x w$ pairs with the $w$ row to complete $\nu (\partial_z u + \partial_x w)^2$, but the hydrostatic energy has no $w$ row, so the cross term stands alone and $\varepsilon_k$ is indefinite pointwise, by a term of order aspect ratio squared relative to $\nu (\partial_z u)^2$, which is the order the hydrostatic approximation neglects elsewhere; the integral is unaffected. KineticEnergyIsotropicDissipationRate is $2\nu S_{ij} S_{ij}$ of the full strain rate and keeps the $w$ row on both model types, so on a hydrostatic model it is not the dissipation term of this budget: it includes the dissipation of $\tfrac{1}{2} w^2$, which the budget does not carry.

Vertically implicit closures

KineticEnergyTendency and KineticEnergyStress wrap Oceananigans' explicit tendency and its viscous term $\partial_j \tau_{ij}$, which apply the closure's time discretization. A closure with a VerticallyImplicitTimeDiscretization, which is CATKE's default and an option of ScalarDiffusivity and VerticalScalarDiffusivity, takes the $\partial_z u$ and $\partial_z v$ parts of its vertical fluxes out of the explicit tendency and applies them in the implicit step, so both diagnostics leave out the vertical viscous work the model does. KineticEnergyDissipationRate is built from the closure's explicit fluxes and keeps it. Under such a closure the terms still rebuild KineticEnergyTendency cell by cell, but that tendency is no longer the change of $e_k$ over a step, the identity $\int \mathrm{DIFF}\, \mathrm{d}V = \int \varepsilon_k\, \mathrm{d}V$ fails, and an integrated budget should be written with $\varepsilon_k$ in place of $\mathrm{DIFF}$. Hydrostatic runs usually carry such a closure, so there this is the common case. The potential energy budget splits the same way, with PotentialEnergyTendency and PotentialEnergyBuoyancyDiffusion on the explicit side and PotentialEnergyDiffusiveVerticalBuoyancyFlux keeping the implicit flux; see the potential energy page.

Immersed boundaries

On an ImmersedBoundaryGrid the momentum tendencies subtract two viscous terms, the interior $\partial_j \tau_{ij}$ and the stress through the immersed faces, which Oceananigans keeps separate. KineticEnergyStress wraps the interior term only, while KineticEnergyTendency carries both. The immersed stress vanishes under the default no-flux immersed boundary conditions, but with an immersed ValueBoundaryCondition or a drag FluxBoundaryCondition on the velocities the budget above is short by the wall-stress work, which no diagnostic here provides; the momentum equation exposes it per component as ImmersedViscousDissipation. KineticEnergyDissipationRate is built from the closure's explicit fluxes without the masking that the interior term applies across immersed faces, so on such a grid it includes shear across faces the model treats as solid and $\int \mathrm{DIFF}\, \mathrm{d}V = \int \varepsilon_k\, \mathrm{d}V$ does not hold.

Tendency

Oceanostics.KineticEnergyEquation.KineticEnergyTendency — Type
KineticEnergyTendency(model; location)

Return a KernelFunctionOperation that computes the tendency uᵢGᵢ of the kinetic energy eₖ, excluding the nonhydrostatic pressure contribution:

∂ₜeₖ = ½∂ₜuᵢ² = uᵢGᵢ - uᵢ∂ᵢpₙₕₛ

On a HydrostaticFreeSurfaceModel the sum runs over u and v only, and Gᵢ is Oceananigans' hydrostatic tendency, which leaves out the part of the barotropic pressure gradient the free surface treats implicitly (all of it for an ImplicitFreeSurface or a SplitExplicitFreeSurface).

On either model Gᵢ is the explicit tendency. A closure with a VerticallyImplicitTimeDiscretization (CATKE's default, and an option of ScalarDiffusivity and VerticalScalarDiffusivity) takes the ∂zu and ∂zv parts of its vertical fluxes out of Gᵢ and applies them in the implicit step, so this term, like KineticEnergyStress, leaves out the vertical viscous work the model does. The change of eₖ over a time step then differs from uᵢGᵢ - uᵢ∂ᵢpₙₕₛ by that work, which DissipationRate still accounts for. On an ImmersedBoundaryGrid Gᵢ also carries the stress through the immersed faces, which KineticEnergyStress leaves out.

Advection

Oceanostics.KineticEnergyEquation.KineticEnergyAdvection — Type
KineticEnergyAdvection(model; velocities, location)

Return a KernelFunctionOperation that computes the advection term, defined as

ADV = uᵢ∂ⱼ(uᵢuⱼ)

On a HydrostaticFreeSurfaceModel it is uₕ⋅(u⃗⋅∇)uₕ over u and v only, built on the kernels (U_dot_∇u, U_dot_∇v) the hydrostatic momentum equations advect with, so it follows the model's scheme, VectorInvariant included. The hydrostatic metric terms that a flux-form scheme picks up on a curvilinear grid are left out, as they are by UMomentumEquation.Advection; they vanish on a RectilinearGrid and for a VectorInvariant scheme.

By default, the advection will be calculated using the resolved velocities and users can use the keyword velocities to modify that behavior:

Stress (diffusive) term

Oceanostics.KineticEnergyEquation.KineticEnergyStress — Type
KineticEnergyStress(model; location)

Return a KernelFunctionOperation that computes the diffusive term of the eₖ prognostic equation:

    DIFF = uᵢ∂ⱼτᵢⱼ

where uᵢ are the velocity components and τᵢⱼ is the viscous momentum flux (minus the stress tensor). It enters the kinetic energy budget as -DIFF, and splits into a transport divergence plus the dissipation rate εₖ (DissipationRate).

A HydrostaticFreeSurfaceModel has no w-momentum equation, so on that model the sum runs over i = 1, 2 only and DIFF is the stress term of the budget of ½(u² + v²). The fluxes τ₁ⱼ and τ₂ⱼ are still computed with the diagnostic w, as in the model's own u and v tendencies.

∂ⱼτᵢⱼ is Oceananigans' own explicit viscous term, with the closure's time discretization applied. A closure with a VerticallyImplicitTimeDiscretization (CATKE's default, and an option of ScalarDiffusivity and VerticalScalarDiffusivity) takes the ∂zu and ∂zv parts of its vertical fluxes out of that term and applies them in the implicit step, so DIFF then leaves out the vertical viscous work the model does, as KineticEnergyTendency does, while DissipationRate keeps the full explicit flux. Under such a closure ∫DIFF dV = ∫εₖ dV no longer holds, and an integrated budget should be written with εₖ rather than with DIFF.

On an ImmersedBoundaryGrid the tendency kernels also subtract the stress through the immersed faces, immersed_∂ⱼτᵢⱼ, which DIFF does not include. It vanishes under the default no-flux immersed boundary conditions, but with an immersed ValueBoundaryCondition or a drag FluxBoundaryCondition on the velocities the wall-stress work is left out of the budget; the momentum modules expose it per component as ImmersedViscousDissipation.

julia> using Oceananigans, Oceanosticsjulia> grid = RectilinearGrid(size=(4, 4, 4), extent=(1, 1, 1));julia> model = NonhydrostaticModel(grid; closure=ScalarDiffusivity(ν=1e-4));julia> DIFF = KineticEnergyEquation.KineticEnergyStress(model)KineticEnergyStress KernelFunctionOperation at (Center, Center, Center)├── grid: 4×4×4 RectilinearGrid{Float64, Periodic, Periodic, Bounded} on CPU with 3×3×3 halo├── kernel_function: uᵢ∂ⱼ_τᵢⱼᶜᶜᶜ (generic function with 1 method)└── arguments: ("NamedTuple", "ScalarDiffusivity", "Nothing", "Clock", "NamedTuple", "Nothing")└── computes: kinetic energy stress/diffusion  uᵢ∂ⱼτᵢⱼ

We can also pass velocities and pressure keywords to perform more specific calculations. The example below illustrates calculation of the nonhydrostatic contribution to the pressure redistrubution term:

julia> ∇u⃗pNHS = KineticEnergyPressureRedistribution(model, pressure=model.pressures.pNHS)KineticEnergyPressureRedistribution KernelFunctionOperation at (Center, Center, Center)├── grid: 1×1×4 RectilinearGrid{Float64, Periodic, Periodic, Bounded} on CPU with 1×1×3 halo├── kernel_function: uᵢ∂ᵢpᶜᶜᶜ (generic function with 1 method)└── arguments: ("NamedTuple", "Field")└── computes: kinetic energy pressure redistribution  uᵢ∂ᵢp

On a HydrostaticFreeSurfaceModel the sum runs over u and v only, and p defaults to the hydrostatic pressure anomaly model.pressure.pHY′, which is the pressure the hydrostatic momentum tendencies carry. The free surface's own pressure is not part of it.

On a NonhydrostaticModel that has taken a time step, Oceananigans (0.106 to 0.116 at least) leaves the halos of pNHS at the pressure times Δt: it corrects the velocities with p Δt and then divides the interior alone by Δt. This term differentiates p across those halos, so in the cells next to a periodic boundary it is wrong until the halos are refilled, with fill_halo_regions!(model.pressures.pNHS) right before it is evaluated. The error is a redistribution and barely changes a volume integral.

Dissipation rate

Oceanostics.KineticEnergyEquation.DissipationRate — Type
DissipationRate(model; U, V, W, location)

Calculate the kinetic energy dissipation rate, defined as

εₖ = -∂ⱼuᵢ ⋅ τᵢⱼ

where ∂ⱼuᵢ is the velocity gradient tensor and τᵢⱼ is the closure's viscous momentum flux (minus the stress tensor), in the same convention Oceananigans uses. That definition holds for any closure. For a Newtonian flux with a scalar viscosity, τᵢⱼ = -2νSᵢⱼ, it reduces on a NonhydrostaticModel to εₖ = 2νSᵢⱼSᵢⱼ ≥ 0, which KineticEnergyIsotropicDissipationRate computes directly; neither that reduction nor, for a flux that is not down-gradient, the sign carries over to other closures.

A HydrostaticFreeSurfaceModel has no w-momentum equation, so on that model the sum runs over i = 1, 2 only and εₖ is the dissipation rate of ½(u² + v²). The fluxes τ₁ⱼ and τ₂ⱼ are still computed with the diagnostic w, as in the model's own u and v tendencies. The reduction to 2νSᵢⱼSᵢⱼ needs the w row, so there εₖ is not that, and the isotropic rate, which keeps the row, is not a term of the hydrostatic budget. ∫εₖ dV is still the viscous sink of ½(u² + v²). Pointwise, εₖ ≥ 0 with a horizontal or vertical formulation (HorizontalScalarDiffusivity, VerticalScalarDiffusivity, CATKE), whose τ is built from u and v alone. With a three-dimensional ScalarDiffusivity, whose τ₁₃ carries ∂ₓw, the cross term ν ∂zu ∂ₓw has no w row to complete its square, so εₖ is indefinite cell by cell, by a term of order aspect ratio squared relative to ν(∂zu)², which is the order the hydrostatic approximation neglects elsewhere.

The fluxes are the closure's explicit viscous_flux_*, with no time discretization applied, so under a closure with a VerticallyImplicitTimeDiscretization (CATKE's default, and an option of ScalarDiffusivity and VerticalScalarDiffusivity) εₖ still includes the vertical shear term that the model applies in the implicit step. KineticEnergyStress and KineticEnergyTendency leave that term out, so ∫DIFF dV = ∫εₖ dV fails there and an integrated budget should be written with εₖ.

On an ImmersedBoundaryGrid the same bare fluxes are not masked across immersed faces, as the _viscous_flux_* behind ∂ⱼτᵢⱼ are, so εₖ there includes the shear across faces the model treats as solid and ∫DIFF dV = ∫εₖ dV does not hold either.

julia> using Oceananigans, Oceanosticsjulia> grid = RectilinearGrid(size=(4, 4, 4), extent=(1, 1, 1));julia> model = NonhydrostaticModel(grid; closure=ScalarDiffusivity(ν=1e-4));julia> ε = KineticEnergyEquation.DissipationRate(model)KineticEnergyDissipationRate KernelFunctionOperation at (Center, Center, Center)├── grid: 4×4×4 RectilinearGrid{Float64, Periodic, Periodic, Bounded} on CPU with 3×3×3 halo├── kernel_function: viscous_dissipation_rate_ccc (generic function with 1 method)└── arguments: ("NamedTuple", "Nothing", "NamedTuple", "NamedTuple")└── computes: kinetic energy dissipation rate  -∂ⱼuᵢ·τᵢⱼ
